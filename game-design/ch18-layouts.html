Clearing the puzzle screen

B+ is option B with panels you can hide, move and resize, which is what you asked for. A, B and C stay for comparison. Each is the same puzzle, playable here: type an arrow, press Test, and find the two lines the matrix does not turn. Same matrix, same answers; only the layout changes. Try each, then compare them at the bottom.

Today

What crowds it

  • Seven separate panels around two small arrows.
  • The same fact (green is (1, 0), yellow is (2, 1)) appears three times.
  • The optional guess sits over the play area.

What it gets right

  • Every number you need is on screen.
  • The dashed line shows the target.

B+ · Your panels

Try this

  • Hide panels (top right, or press I) clears everything off the picture. Press it again to bring them back.
  • Each panel's – hides just that panel; the Panels menu brings any of them back.
  • Drag a panel by its title bar to move it.
  • Drag the bottom-right corner to resize it.
  • Your layout is remembered. Reset layout puts everything back.

Also changed from B

  • Vectors are columns: the input boxes for x stack top to bottom.
  • Yellow's label carries the angle (Ax = (6, 3) · 27° off), so there is one label per arrow.
  • The numbers on the arrows can be switched off in the Panels menu.

A · One panel

What changes

  • Everything you read or type sits in one column, top to bottom, in the order you use it.
  • The picture gets two thirds of the screen and zooms to fit your arrows.
  • Each fact appears once. The guess is one line, not a pop-up.
  • Chapter title shrinks; five top buttons become one menu.

Trade-off

Your eyes move between the picture and the column. The column gets long on a small laptop screen.

B · On the picture

What changes

  • The numbers sit on the arrows: green says x = (1, 0), yellow says Ax = (2, 1), the angle sits between them.
  • One task line at the top, one command bar at the bottom. No side panels.
  • When yellow lands on the line, the question pops up right next to it.
  • The full explanation is behind the i button.

Trade-off

Labels can crowd when arrows are short or lie on top of each other. The long explanation is one click away instead of always showing.

C · One step at a time

What changes

  • Only the current step is on screen: guess, try an arrow, line 1, line 2, why.
  • The guess comes first and never covers the picture.
  • Each step says only what that step needs.
  • The "why" is its own last step, so it is never skipped.

Trade-off

Less freedom to jump ahead, and more clicks for a player who already knows what to do.

My pick: B, with C's order the first time a puzzle opens. The numbers belong where your eyes already are, on the arrows. Showing the guess first, then the play, then the why, keeps the screen quiet without hiding anything. Your call decides how every puzzle in the game gets laid out.

Best viewed on a wide screen; on a phone the game screens shrink to fit.
